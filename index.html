Personalised Resistance Training Consortium

From variability to precision in resistance training

An international research collaboration building the conceptual, methodological, and analytical foundations for personalised resistance training prescription practice — starting from a simple but overlooked idea: what is written on a training plan and what the body actually receives are not the same thing.

Framework

A prescription is not a dose

Two people being prescribed the same sets, reps, and load intensity scheme can experience a very different internal training stimulus. Resistance training research has long treated the numbers on a training plan as if they were the biological dose itself. We start from a different premise, borrowed from medicine and epidemiology: what is prescribed, what is actually performed, and the internal stimulus that drives adaptation are distinct steps in a causal chain — and each needs to be defined, measured, and studied on its own terms.

1 · Treatment

What is prescribed

The programme as written: sets, load, rest, tempo, and technique targets.

2 · Exposure

What is performed

The training actually completed on the day, including everything that deviates from the plan.

3 · Dose

What the body receives

The internal mechanical, metabolic, and perceptual stimulus this creates — not directly observable, but inferable from proxies such as impulse.

4 · Response

What follows

The acute and chronic adaptation that results, in both direction and magnitude.

What we work on

Four connected lines of work

Our work follows the treatment–exposure–dose–response pathway from conceptualisation through to practice.

A

Conceptual foundations

Clarifying what “dose” means in resistance training, and identifying valid proxies for a stimulus we cannot measure directly at the muscle level.

B

Trial design

Implementing appropriate study designs to investigate causal links along the treatment–exposure–dose–response pathway.

C

Analytical methods

Leveraging applied statistics to separate real, stable within&ndash and between&ndashindividual delivered doses and responses from ordinary day-to-day noise and measurement error.

D

Autoregulation paradigm

Testing whether dynamically adjusting training to an individual’s current state — rather than to a fixed target — delivers a more consistent stimulus over time.

Foundational work

Where the framework comes from

P

From Variability to Precision: A Conceptual Framework, Methodological Considerations and Analytical Approaches in Personalised Resistance Training Prescription

Antonio Dello Iacono, Ivan Jukic, Paul Swinton

Method article · Preprint · Research Square · 2026

This paper sets out the treatment–exposure–dose–response framework, maps the sources of variability in delivered training dose, and shows why replicate crossover designs and variance-decomposition modelling are needed to support genuine personalisation. It forms the conceptual basis for the Consortium’s work.
